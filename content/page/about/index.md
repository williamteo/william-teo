Hi, I am William. I work on strategy and skill learning for multi-robot
systems.

A single robot can be trained to do a hard thing. A team has to solve a harder
problem: decide who does what, act on what the others are likely to do, and
keep improving while the others are also improving. It also has to detect what
another agent intends and react to it, whether that agent is a dynamic obstacle
or actively adversarial. I approach these problems through reinforcement
learning and game theory.

Representation learning runs underneath. What an agent learns to notice, and
what it abstracts away, sets the limit on what it can coordinate on and how far
a learned skill carries to a new task. I am interested in which
representations survive that transfer.

## Skill acquisition, in and out of the lab

The same questions turn up away from the lab. I skateboard and I learn
languages. In both, the interesting part is how raw practice turns into skill,
and why some hours of practice count for far more than others. Deliberate
practice, curriculum, and the structure of feedback are, I think, the same
problems a learning agent faces, described in a different vocabulary.

## Mathematics

I read topology, graph theory, category theory, group theory, and geometry for
my own interest. What draws me is the structure they expose: symmetries and
invariants sitting beneath things that look unrelated on the surface.
